Puppy with an inguinal hernia: rest, or operate now?

■ decision node ● chance node ▲ outcome (terminal) node

🐶 A puppy is diagnosed with an inguinal hernia

The vet is choosing between two initial plans: resting the puppy for 4 weeks to see if the hernia closes on its own, or going straight to surgery.

However, surgery gets more expensive the bigger the puppy gets, since a heavier puppy needs more anaesthetic and a longer procedure.

Plan 1 — Rest 4 weeks

  • Costs ฿400 for a re-examination visit to check on the hernia.
  • 40% chance the hernia has resolved by the check-up — if so, no surgery is needed.
  • If it hasn't resolved, the vet extends rest for 3 more weeks, with another ฿400 re-examination visit.
  • 30% chance of recovery at that follow-up — again, no surgery needed if so.
  • If it's still not resolved, surgery is needed. Because the puppy is heavier now, it costs ฿7,500 at this stage.
  • Surgery succeeds 75% of the time. If it fails, an extra ฿1,600 in follow-up care is needed.

Plan 2 — Go straight to surgery

  • Costs ฿4,500 while the puppy is still small.
  • Succeeds 90% of the time. If it fails, an extra ฿1,600 in follow-up care is needed.
