Partial Budget Analysis: Herd Health Economics Exercise

For: Bachelor veterinary students  ·  Purpose: Practice using partial budgeting to evaluate farm-level management decisions

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this exercise, students should be able to:

  • Explain what a partial budget is and when to use it
  • Identify the four components of a partial budget
  • Calculate line-item values from raw farm data
  • Make a recommendation and justify it

2. What Is a Partial Budget?

A partial budget estimates the financial effect of a small, specific change in a farm enterprise. It only asks: if we make this one change, what happens to profit?

Increases profitDecreases profit
A. Added revenue — new income gained from the change C. Reduced revenue — income lost because of the change
B. Reduced costs — costs avoided because of the change D. Added costs — new costs from the change

Formula:

Net change in profit = (A + B) − (C + D)

Common pitfalls to avoid

  • Double counting: an effect should appear in only one cell, not two.
  • Ignoring opportunity cost: if a resource (e.g., labor, barn space) is freed up or tied up, its value counts even if no cash changes hands.
  • Treating uncertain outcomes as certain: when data describe a probability or a risk, that must be reflected (expected value), not just the best case.

3. Practice Scenario 1: Selective Dry Cow Therapy

Practice — Solve It

A 90-cow dairy herd currently gives blanket dry cow antibiotic therapy to all cows at dry-off. The vet proposes switching to selective dry cow therapy (SDCT): only cows with somatic cell count (SCC) above 200,000 cells/mL at the last test receive antibiotics; the rest receive an internal teat sealant only.

Raw data provided:

  • 90 cows are dried off per year; historically, 55% have SCC above the 200,000 threshold
  • Antibiotic dry tube cost: 180 THB/cow; teat sealant cost: 90 THB/cow (every cow gets one or the other, not both)
  • SDCT requires an extra SCC test per cow before dry-off at 40 THB/cow (currently not done)
  • Herd veterinarian estimates that leaving low-SCC cows on sealant-only (instead of antibiotics) causes 2 additional clinical mastitis cases per year in the first 30 days of the next lactation, each costing 3,200 THB in treatment and discarded milk
  1. Calculate how many cows fall into each treatment group under SDCT.
  2. Enter the total amount (THB/year) for each category below.
  3. Calculate the net change in profit and give a recommendation.
Click to see hint

Two separate added costs apply under SDCT: the SCC test (charged on all 90 cows, since every cow needs testing to sort into groups) and the extra mastitis cases (only in the low-SCC group). Don't forget either one when calculating D. There is no change to revenue (A or C) in this scenario.

CategoryWhat to includeAmount (THB/year)
A. Added revenue—
B. Reduced costDrug cost saved by switching some cows to sealant
C. Reduced revenue—
D. Added costSCC testing + extra mastitis cases combined

4. Practice Scenario 2: Automated Calf Feeder

Practice — Solve It

A calf-rearing operation (40 calves/year) is considering leasing an automated milk feeder to replace manual bucket feeding.

Raw data provided:

  • Annual lease fee for the automated feeder: 60,000 THB/year
  • Annual maintenance and electricity for the feeder: 12,000 THB/year
  • Current manual feeding requires 1.5 hours/day of paid labor at 80 THB/hour, 365 days/year; automated feeding reduces this to 20 minutes/day for monitoring only
  • Calves on the automated feeder gain weight faster due to more frequent small meals: average weaning weight increases by 4 kg/calf, and each kg of extra weaning weight is worth an estimated 65 THB in reduced time-to-first-breeding value
  • Milk replacer cost per calf is unchanged between systems
  1. Convert the labor and weight-gain data into annual THB values.
  2. Enter the total amount (THB/year) for each category below.
  3. The manager also asks: "What if maintenance costs turn out to be 50% higher than estimated? Does the recommendation change?" Briefly answer.
Click to see hint

Convert everything to a per-year basis before adding it up: labor cost = hours/day × wage × 365; weight-gain value = kg gained × value per kg × number of calves/year. Add the lease fee and maintenance together for the total added cost.

CategoryWhat to includeAmount (THB/year)
A. Added revenueValue of extra weaning weight gain
B. Reduced costLabor cost saved (manual minus automated)
C. Reduced revenue—
D. Added costLease fee + maintenance/electricity

5. Practice Scenario 3: Herd Vaccination Against a Sporadic Disease (Expected Value)

Practice — Solve It

A 120-cow herd in an area with occasional outbreaks of a reproductive disease is deciding whether to vaccinate the whole herd annually. This scenario requires working with probability, since the disease does not occur every year.

Raw data provided:

  • Vaccine cost: 220 THB/cow/year, plus 15 THB/cow for labor to administer (120 cows)
  • Based on regional surveillance data, an outbreak occurs in an unvaccinated herd of this size in approximately 1 of every 5 years
  • When an outbreak occurs, historical farm records show an average of 18 cows abort, each abortion costing an estimated 22,000 THB (lost calf, treatment, extended days open, cull risk)
  • Vaccination is estimated to reduce outbreak-related abortions by 80% in years an outbreak occurs, but does not affect the probability that an outbreak occurs elsewhere in the region
  • No effect on milk yield or normal reproduction in non-outbreak years
  1. Calculate the expected annual cost of abortion losses without vaccination (probability × cost, averaged per year).
  2. Calculate the expected annual cost of abortion losses with vaccination.
  3. Use the difference as the reduced-cost (B) line item, alongside the vaccination cost (D).
  4. Enter the total amount (THB/year) for each category below, then the net change and recommendation.
  5. Discuss: how would your recommendation change if outbreaks occurred 1 in every 10 years instead of 1 in 5?
Click to see hint

Expected cost = probability of an outbreak in a given year × average loss when an outbreak occurs. Calculate this twice — once using the unvaccinated abortion count (18 cows), once using the vaccinated abortion count (18 cows reduced by 80%) — then take the difference as your reduced-cost line.

CategoryWhat to includeAmount (THB/year)
A. Added revenue—
B. Reduced costExpected abortion loss avoided by vaccinating
C. Reduced revenue—
D. Added costVaccine + labor to administer, all 120 cows
