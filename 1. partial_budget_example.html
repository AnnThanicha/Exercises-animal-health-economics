Partial Budget Analysis — Worked Example

A simple, fully-solved illustration showing all four quadrants of a partial budget before students attempt the practice scenarios.

What Is a Partial Budget?

A partial budget estimates the financial effect of a small, specific change in a farm enterprise. It only asks: if we make this one change, what happens to profit?

Increases profitDecreases profit
A. Added revenue — new income gained from the change C. Reduced revenue — income lost because of the change
B. Reduced costs — costs avoided because of the change D. Added costs — new costs from the change

Formula:

Net change in profit = (A + B) − (C + D)

Worked Example

Fully Solved

Scenario: A 60-cow dairy herd currently uses a bull for natural service breeding. The farm is considering switching entirely to artificial insemination (AI) using purchased semen.

ItemCategoryAmount (THB/year)
Higher milk yield from improved geneticsA. Added revenue35,000
No longer feeding and housing the bullB. Reduced cost20,000
Lost stud fee income (neighboring farms paid to use the bull)C. Reduced revenue8,000
AI service visits and semen strawsD. Added cost40,000

Calculation:

Net change = (35,000 + 20,000) − (8,000 + 40,000)
           = 55,000 − 48,000
           = +7,000 THB/year
Net change in profit: +7,000 THB/year

Interpretation: Switching to AI is expected to increase annual profit by 7,000 THB — a modest but positive gain. Notice that all four quadrants had a real value here: the change created new revenue (A), avoided a cost (B), gave up an old revenue source (C), and introduced a new cost (D). A recommendation should also weigh non-financial factors not captured in the numbers, such as genetic improvement and reduced disease transmission risk from natural service.
