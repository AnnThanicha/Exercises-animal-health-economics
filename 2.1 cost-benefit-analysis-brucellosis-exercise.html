Cost-Benefit Analysis: Brucellosis Control — Vaccination vs. Test-and-Slaughter

Work through real 8-year program data to compare two national disease-control strategies, then decide which one you'd recommend.

Learning objective: calculate and compare NPV and benefit-cost ratio (BCR) for two competing animal-health interventions, using real published program data, and practice choosing between them under different decision criteria.

Scenario: Brucellosis control in Bosnia and Herzegovina, 2009–2016

Small ruminant brucellosis (Brucella melitensis) had become a major animal and human health problem in Bosnia and Herzegovina. In 2009, the national veterinary authority launched an 8-year control program in sheep and goats, comparing two strategies against doing nothing: mass vaccination with the Rev 1 vaccine, and test-and-slaughter (testing the herd and culling infected animals). Below are the program's actual annual costs and benefits (avoided losses in animals, avoided human cases, and avoided costs of the previous control approach), all in Euros. Work out the discounted cash flow for each strategy, then compare them.

Before you start How the costs and benefits are estimated

The Year 1 and Year 2 figures used later in this exercise are not arbitrary — they come from straightforward formulas applied to real program data. Work through the two calculations below so you understand where the numbers in the main tables come from, before treating them as given.

Cost estimation: vaccination program

Each year, the vaccination cost has two parts: the cost of vaccinating the covered population (dose + veterinary/administration + other per-animal costs), plus the cost of sampling and testing a small share of the population alongside vaccination. A separate set of fixed costs (public awareness, training, monitoring, office running costs, and the cold chain) is added on top, and does not depend on population size.

Given inputs
  • Sheep population: 830,000    Goat population: 53,000
  • Vaccine cost per animal: 0.135 EUR
  • Veterinary/administration cost per animal: 1.55 EUR
  • Other cost per animal: 0.10 EUR
  • Share of population also sampled and tested alongside vaccination: 5%
  • Sampling cost per animal tested: 2.55 EUR
  • Testing cost per animal tested: 2.55 EUR
  • Vaccination coverage: 83.3% in Year 1 (first mass campaign), 20.2% in Year 2 onward (covering new and previously unvaccinated animals)
  • Formula: vaccination cost = population × coverage × (vaccine cost + veterinary cost + other cost) + population × tested share × (sampling cost + testing cost)

Cost estimation: test-and-slaughter

Test-and-slaughter has a simpler cost structure than vaccination: no separate fixed program costs, and no coverage rate to phase in. Each year, a fixed share of the population is sampled and tested, and any animal found infected is culled — the farm is paid to replace it, and the carcass is destroyed.

Given inputs
  • Sheep population: 830,000    Goat population: 53,000
  • Share of population sampled, tested, and culled each year: 50%
  • Sampling cost per animal tested: 2.55 EUR
  • Testing cost per animal tested: 2.55 EUR
  • Replacement cost per culled animal: 120 EUR
  • Destruction cost per culled animal: 10 EUR
  • Prevalence (from the epidemiological model), falling by 37.5% each year under testing and culling: 4.5% in Year 1, ≈2.81% in Year 2, ≈1.76% in Year 3
  • Formula: test-and-slaughter cost = population × tested share × (sampling cost + testing cost) + population × tested share × prevalence × (replacement cost + destruction cost)

Benefit estimation: avoided human cases

Human health benefits come from brucellosis cases that don't happen because the herd is under control. Each avoided case saves the costs it would otherwise have caused: lost income during illness, hospitalization, treatment, and other costs to the affected family. This benefit is the same whether the herd is controlled by vaccination or by test-and-slaughter, since either strategy reduces human exposure by a similar amount.

Given inputs
  • Income loss due to sick leave: 700 EUR/case
  • Hospitalization: 450 EUR/case
  • Treatment/medication: 500 EUR/case
  • Other family costs: 300 EUR/case
  • Avoided human cases (from the model), compared to the rising trend under no control: 0 in Year 1, 403 in Year 2, 743 in Year 3
  • Formula: cost per human case = income loss + hospitalization + treatment/medication + other family costs

Benefit estimation: avoided animal losses

The animal-side benefit works the same way in principle as the human one: a reduction in disease prevalence means fewer abortions and less milk lost to illness, each of which has a Euro value. The paper's formula is:

Formula (per species)
  • Avoided abortion value = ΔP × population × reduction in reproduction from infection × reproduction rate × price per young animal
  • Avoided milk-loss value = ΔP × population × reduction in milk yield from infection × milk yield × milk price
where ΔP is the fall in prevalence that year, compared to what prevalence would have been without any control.

Working out ΔP itself requires simulating the year-by-year spread of disease through the herd (an epidemiological model), which goes beyond a hand calculation — so it's given below. Everything else in the formula, you calculate yourself.

Given inputs
  • Sheep population: 830,000    Goat population: 53,000
  • Reduction in reproduction from infection: 32.2%    Reduction in milk yield from infection: 15%
  • Reproduction rate: 1.2 lambs/ewe/yr (sheep), 1.5 kids/doe/yr (goats)
  • Price per young animal: 80 EUR (lamb), 90 EUR (kid)
  • Milk yield: 60 L/ewe/yr (sheep), 115 L/doe/yr (goats)
  • Milk price: 0.609 EUR/L (sheep), 1.307 EUR/L (goats)
  • ΔP (from the epidemiological model): 0% in Year 1 (too early for the vaccination effect to show up), ≈1.93% in Year 2, ≈4.03% in Year 3
  • Avoided abortion value — sheep = ΔP × sheep population × reduction in reproduction × sheep reproduction rate × price per lamb
  • Avoided milk-loss value — sheep = ΔP × sheep population × reduction in milk yield × sheep milk yield × sheep milk price
  • Avoided abortion value — goats = ΔP × goat population × reduction in reproduction × goat reproduction rate × price per kid
  • Avoided milk-loss value — goats = ΔP × goat population × reduction in milk yield × goat milk yield × goat milk price
  • Total avoided animal-loss benefit = sum of the four values above
